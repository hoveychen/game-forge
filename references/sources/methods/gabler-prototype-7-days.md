# How to Prototype a Game in Under 7 Days (Experimental Gameplay Project)

- URL: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/pc/feature-how-to-prototype-a-game-in-under-7-days- (copy: https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~markk/SWE_copies/gabler_prototyping.html); GDC 2006 talk https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1013294/How-to-Prototype-a-Game
- Authors: Kyle Gabler, Kyle Gray, Shalin Shodhan, Matt Kucic (CMU Experimental Gameplay Project; later World of Goo / 2D Boy)
- Date: Gamasutra, Oct 2005; GDC 2006
- Why it matters: canonical "find the fun" practice from a team that shipped 50 prototypes in a semester (Tower of Goo -> World of Goo). Toy-first, cut losses, theming cannot save bad design -- the opposite of agents polishing a broken or unfun core.

## Rules (verbatim headings / lines)
- "Embrace the Possibility of Failure - it Encourages Creative Risk Taking"
- "Enforce Short Development Cycles (More Time != More Quality)"
- "Constrain Creativity to Make You Want it Even More"
- "Formal Brainstorming Has a 0% Success Rate"
- "Gather Concept Art and Music to Create an Emotional Target"
- "Simulate in Your Head – Pre-Prototype the Prototype"
- Build the toy first: "the 'toy' should be the core mechanic of the game minus any goals or decisions."
- "If You Can Get Away With it, Fake it"
- "Cut Your Losses"
- "Heavy Theming Will Not Salvage Bad Design"
- "Apply a Healthy Spread of Art, Sound, and Music"
- "Complexity is Not Necessary for Fun"
- "Build Toward a Well Defined Goal"
- Make it juicy: constant feedback via animation, sound, responsive elements.

## Mapping
- Session 1-N gate: a "toy" that is satisfying to poke with no goals. If the toy isn't fun, the fix is at the mechanic level, not the content/UI level.
- Explicit rule for agents: no theming/art/content work while the toy/core-loop gate is failing.
