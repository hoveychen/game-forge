# Twenty Years, Twenty Lessons (Mark Rosewater, Magic: The Gathering)

- URL: https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/twenty-years-twenty-lessons-part-3-2016-06-13 ; GDC 2016 talk https://gdcvault.com/play/1023186/Twenty-Years-Twenty
- Author/org: Mark Rosewater, Wizards of the Coast
- Date: GDC 2016; article 2016-06-13
- Why it matters: the most-cited designer rule for interpreting playtest feedback -- players are right about the PROBLEM, not about the SOLUTION. Directly counters agents "implementing the literal suggestion" (superficial change) instead of diagnosing the cause.

- "Lesson #15: Design the component for its intended audience"
- "Lesson #16: Be more afraid of boring your players than challenging them"

- "Lesson #19: Your audience is good at recognizing problems and bad at solving them"

## Lesson 19 content (fetcher-extracted; original is longer, verify wording before quoting publicly)
"Your players have a better understanding of how they feel about your game than you do. They can tell easier when something is wrong and they're excellent at identifying problems, but they're not as equipped to solve the problems. They don't know the restrictions you're under or what needs you have to fulfill. ... So use your audience as a resource to help figure out what is wrong with your game, but take it with a grain of salt when they offer you solutions."
Framed with a doctor analogy: the doctor asks how you feel, not what treatment to prescribe.

## Mapping
Feedback handling protocol for agents: (1) restate each piece of feedback as an observed feeling/problem, (2) hypothesize root cause in the mechanic/loop, (3) choose a solution possibly different from the player's suggestion, (4) state how the next playtest will verify it. Lesson 17 supports that a small root-cause change can matter more than many surface changes.
